This project puts forth a method and implementation for ardupilot of optimal control for tilt rotor Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAV) landing transitions (specifically the Arace Griffin Pro) to reduce the energy consumption and  landing period by $61\%$ and $51\%$ respectivly compared to the stock ArduPilot  approach. As the use of small and medium scale powered lift UAVs increases in commercial settings so too does the demand for greater endurance and efficiency. 
This is generally approached from a hardware perspective, through higher density batteries, more efficient propulsion or lighter  and more efficient air frames. However this project attempts to solve this through exploiting the dynamics of the  aircrafts to reduce control effort, through a optimal control approach. Trajectory and control optimisations for UAVs is not a new area of research, however, this project provides an new and easier way to implement this for end users, as the control is integrated into ArduPilot, an existing and widespread approach to autonomous control of UAVs. This project specifically looks at the Arace Griffin Pro tilt rotor.
	This thesis starts by putting forth a longitudinal dynamical model of tilt rotor aircraft, by combining previous models of multirotor and fixed-wing aircraft. Followed by a method of trajectory optimisation, control and simulation. 

Powered lift UAVs are a class of aircraft that have two separate flight modes. The first being fixed-wing flight like that of a conventional plane, the second being hover, similar to a multi-rotor. The combination of these two modes mean that the aircraft can takeoff and land without specialised infrastructure such as a runway, while being able to cruise in the more efficient fixed-wing flight mode, as the rotors in the fixed-wing mode only have to impart enough energy to balance the drag force, while in hover the rotors have to overcome the gravitational force of the entire airframe. Powered lift UAVs achieve this in different ways, some have separate propellers for hover and fixed-wing flight, some tilt their propellers from vertical to horizontal and some even tilt the entire wing with propellers statically mounted. These different airframe types all different approaches to transitioning between flight modes, but this project specifically looks at the Arace Griffin Pro a tilt rotor aircraft (Figure 1.1).
