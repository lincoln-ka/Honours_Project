The solution of the trajectory optimisation can be found in \ref{fig:Open-Loop States} \& \ref{fig:Open-Loop Control}, where you can see the aircraft initially enters a dive, riding the negative pitch constraint ($-20 \, deg$) until an altitude of $17 \, m$. At which point the aircraft pulls up, briefly hitting the positive pitch rate constraint ($15 \, deg/s$), until stall is achieved, which is subsequently held till the aircraft reaches the termination condition. This results in a total energy consumption of $4536 \, J$ (This can't be compared directly with the results in \ref{fig:Landing Approach Energy Consumption} as this value doesn't include the energy associated with the final hover phase as that is not part of the trajectory optimisation).
The inputs for the optimised trajectory (\ref{fig:Open-Loop Control}) remained bounded throughout the approach and are absent of any oscillatory behaviour, providing a smooth feedforward term for the controller to track.

Another approach for analysing the trajectory is the energy states. To land the aircraft has two objectives, bleeding off the kinetic and potential energy. The solution trajectory immediately converts the potential energy to kinetic through the dive in the first 8s of the approach, followed by a pull up into a stall, that hits the pitch rate constraint causing the gain in height, however this stall also dissipates the vast majority of the kinetic energy, which remains largely constant in final stage of the approach (12s-30.5s) as the aircraft holds the upper angle of attack constraint ($20\deg$) and therefore a stall stabilised with the quad rotors to slowly approach the hover for handover.

\section{Control Results}
The longitudinal LQR controller prioritises tracking the positions over the associated velocities as seen in figures \ref{fig:x_pair} through \ref{fig:theta_pair}. This trade off was made on the basis of a small error in velocities would be less detrimental than a error in position, for example during the initial dive towards the ground as seen in first 8s of the landing approach as position overshoot could result in the aircraft nosediving into the ground especially if this controller was run with a trajectory that prioritised efficiency more and had the termination condition at ground level rather than a 10m offset. The aircraft pitch \ref{fig:theta_pair} does track far worse than the other two position in the LQR, that is due to the coupling of pitch to altitude and longitudinal position, and the controller prioritising the other two over pitch. This tradeoff was also made in the lateral controller 
 as seen in the difference of tracking performance between \ref{fig:y_pair} and \ref{fig:phi_pair}, \ref{fig:psi_pair}.

Further improvement could be made to the lateral control by incorporating the rudder and aileron into the script controller, this would also have the added benefit of relaxing the constraints on the waypoints in ArduPilot, as the current solution requires a waypoint that lies on the line drawn between the VTOL Landing and the previous waypoint to be the trajectory $x[0]-waypoint_{radius}$  from the landing waypoint. if it lies off the line the ArduPilot driven control surfaces, primarily the ailerons will drive the aircraft off track.

The other benefit of incorporating the full lateral dynamics would be that the lateral control could be achieved through the LQR which would provide a more optimal solution to the disturbance rejection control. Additionally if the lateral dynamics were integrated this controller would capable of driving any arbitrary trajectory, widening the possible applications.
